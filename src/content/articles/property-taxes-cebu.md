# Property Taxes in Cebu 2026: Complete Guide to Real Estate Taxes, Rates and Fees

If you own property or plan to buy a house, condominium, or residential lot in Cebu, understanding property taxes is essential.

Real estate ownership involves several taxes and government fees. Some are paid annually, while others become due when you purchase, sell, donate, or transfer property.

Whether you are a homeowner, landlord, property investor, or overseas Filipino worker (OFW), knowing how property taxes in Cebu work can help you prepare your budget and avoid unnecessary penalties.

This guide explains the major real estate taxes in Cebu, how they are calculated, who pays them, and what property owners should know about the applicable government requirements.

## What Are the Different Property Taxes in Cebu?

Property owners and buyers may encounter several types of taxes and fees.

The most important include:

| Tax or Fee | When It Applies |
|---|---|
| Real Property Tax (RPT) | Annual property ownership |
| Special Education Fund (SEF) Tax | Annual property ownership |
| Capital Gains Tax (CGT) | Certain property sales |
| Documentary Stamp Tax (DST) | Sale or transfer documents |
| Local Transfer Tax | Transfer of property ownership |
| Registration Fees | Registration of property transfer |
| Estate Tax | Transfer of property through inheritance |
| Donor's Tax | Transfer of property through donation |
| VAT | Certain property sales by businesses |

Not every property transaction is subject to all these taxes.

The applicable taxes depend on the type of transaction, classification of the property, seller's tax status, and location.

## 1. Real Property Tax in Cebu

Real Property Tax, commonly called RPT or real estate tax, is an annual local tax imposed on real properties.

It generally applies to:

- Residential land
- Commercial land
- Industrial properties
- Houses and buildings
- Condominium units
- Other taxable improvements

Property owners pay RPT to the local government unit (LGU) where the property is located.

For example:

- Properties in Cebu City pay through Cebu City.
- Properties in Mandaue pay through Mandaue City.
- Properties in Lapu-Lapu pay through Lapu-Lapu City.
- Properties in Talisay pay through Talisay City.
- Properties in Minglanilla pay through the Municipality of Minglanilla.

Cebu does not have one universal real property tax computation for every city and municipality.

### How Is Real Property Tax Calculated?

Real Property Tax is calculated using the assessed value of the property.

The basic formula is:

**Assessed Value = Fair Market Value × Assessment Level**

Then:

**Basic Real Property Tax = Assessed Value × Applicable Local Tax Rate**

The Special Education Fund tax is calculated separately.

### Example of Real Property Tax Computation

Assume you own a residential property with the following illustrative values:

| Property Information | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Fair Market Value | ₱3,000,000 |
| Residential Assessment Level | 20% |
| Assessed Value | ₱600,000 |

Assume a basic RPT rate of 2% and an SEF rate of 1%.

Basic RPT:

₱600,000 × 2% = ₱12,000

SEF:

₱600,000 × 1% = ₱6,000

Total annual property tax:

**₱18,000**

<text>This is an illustrative computation, not a confirmed tax assessment for a particular Cebu property. Actual fair market values, assessment levels, local rates, exemptions, and applicable adjustments must be checked with the local Assessor and Treasurer.</text>

## 2. Special Education Fund (SEF) Tax

The Special Education Fund tax is an additional annual property tax imposed under the Local Government Code.

It supports local education-related programs and facilities.

The statutory SEF levy is generally 1% of the assessed value of taxable real property.

Using the previous example:

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Assessed Value | ₱600,000 |
| SEF Rate | 1% |
| Annual SEF | ₱6,000 |

This tax is generally collected together with Real Property Tax.

## 3. Property Tax Rates in Cebu: What Buyers Should Know

Under the Local Government Code, the statutory maximum basic RPT rates are:

| Location | Maximum Basic RPT Rate |
|---|---:|
| Province | 1% of assessed value |
| City | 2% of assessed value |
| Municipality within Metro Manila | 2% of assessed value |
| Special Education Fund | Additional 1% |

These are statutory limits, not a statement that every Cebu LGU charges the maximum.

The actual applicable rates must be confirmed with the relevant local government.

### Cebu City Property Tax

Cebu City has been updating its property valuation framework under Republic Act No. 12001, or the Real Property Valuation and Assessment Reform Act.

The city published a proposed 2027 Schedule of Market Values in March 2026 for public consultation.

This is important because property valuation affects assessed values and therefore potential real property tax liabilities.

Buyers and existing property owners should distinguish between:

- Market selling price
- Fair Market Value for assessment
- Assessed Value
- Actual tax due

These are not necessarily identical.

### Property Tax in Mandaue City

Property owners in Mandaue should obtain their applicable assessment information from the Mandaue City Assessor's Office.

Check the property's classification, assessed value, applicable tax rates, and any local adjustments.

### Property Tax in Lapu-Lapu City

Lapu-Lapu property owners should verify their annual RPT computation with the City Assessor and City Treasurer.

Condominiums, residential houses, and commercial properties may have different assessment classifications.

### Property Tax in Talisay City

Property owners should check their latest Tax Declaration and local assessment records.

Do not assume that the rates and assessed values used in Cebu City also apply in Talisay.

### Property Tax in Minglanilla and Other Municipalities

Properties outside the cities may fall under provincial and municipal tax arrangements.

Confirm the applicable basic RPT and SEF collection procedures with the local Treasurer's Office.

## 4. Capital Gains Tax When Selling Property in Cebu

Capital Gains Tax is a national tax that generally applies when an individual sells Philippine real estate classified as a capital asset.

The applicable rate is generally:

**6% of the higher of:**
- Gross selling price; or
- Current fair market value or applicable government valuation under the tax rules.

The applicable BIR zonal valuation is an important consideration in the computation.

### Example of Capital Gains Tax

Assume a property is sold under these conditions:

| Information | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Selling Price | ₱5,000,000 |
| Applicable Government Valuation | ₱5,500,000 |
| Tax Basis | ₱5,500,000 |
| Capital Gains Tax Rate | 6% |

Computation:

₱5,500,000 × 6% = ₱330,000

Estimated Capital Gains Tax:

**₱330,000**

Capital Gains Tax is generally the seller's tax for a transaction involving property classified as a capital asset, although the parties should clearly document their agreed allocation of expenses.

Important: Property held as an ordinary asset by a real estate developer or a person engaged in the real estate business may follow different tax rules.

## 5. Documentary Stamp Tax (DST)

Documentary Stamp Tax is imposed on certain documents and instruments involving the sale, transfer, or conveyance of real property.

For a typical taxable sale of real property, the general DST rate is:

**1.5% of the higher applicable tax base.**

### Example of Documentary Stamp Tax

Assume:

| Information | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Selling Price | ₱5,000,000 |
| Applicable Government Valuation | ₱5,500,000 |
| Tax Basis | ₱5,500,000 |
| DST Rate | 1.5% |

Computation:

₱5,500,000 × 1.5% = ₱82,500

Estimated Documentary Stamp Tax:

**₱82,500**

The responsible party should be identified in the purchase agreement and checked against the applicable tax rules.

## 6. Local Transfer Tax in Cebu

Transfer Tax is collected by the local government when ownership of real property is transferred.

The actual rate depends on the applicable local government ordinance.

Under the Local Government Code:

- A province may impose a transfer tax of up to 0.5%.
- A city may impose a rate up to 0.75%.

These are statutory ceilings, not universal Cebu rates.

### Example of Transfer Tax

Assume a property transfer has a taxable basis of ₱5 million and the applicable local rate is 0.5%.

Computation:

₱5,000,000 × 0.5% = ₱25,000

Estimated Transfer Tax:

**₱25,000**

The actual amount should be confirmed with the Treasurer's Office of the city or municipality where the property is located.

## 7. Registration Fees

After completing the tax requirements, the property transfer must be registered with the Registry of Deeds.

Registration fees are separate from the national and local taxes.

The amount depends on the applicable registration fee schedule and transaction details.

The buyer should obtain a complete estimate before processing the transfer.

## 8. Estate Tax When Inheriting Property in Cebu

Estate Tax applies to the transfer of a deceased person's estate to heirs and beneficiaries.

The general Philippine estate tax rate is:

**6% of the net taxable estate.**

The taxable estate is determined after applying the allowable deductions and exemptions under the law.

Estate Tax is not automatically calculated using the selling price of a single inherited property.

### Example

Assume an illustrative net taxable estate of ₱8 million.

Estate Tax:

₱8,000,000 × 6% = ₱480,000

Estimated Estate Tax:

**₱480,000**

The actual computation depends on the complete estate, applicable deductions, and tax rules.

## 9. Donor's Tax When Donating Property

Donor's Tax applies when real property is transferred as a gift during the donor's lifetime.

The general donor's tax rate is:

**6% of the total net gifts exceeding the applicable ₱250,000 annual exemption.**

For example, assume an individual makes a taxable net gift of ₱3 million.

Estimated Donor's Tax:

(₱3,000,000 - ₱250,000) × 6%

= ₱165,000

Actual tax liability depends on the donor's total net gifts during the calendar year and applicable valuation rules.

## 10. VAT on Real Estate Transactions

Value-Added Tax may apply to certain sales of real property by businesses, depending on the seller's classification, property type, transaction value, and applicable exemptions.

This is particularly relevant when purchasing property directly from a developer or real estate business.

A transaction involving property classified as an ordinary asset may have different tax treatment from the sale of a private individual's capital asset.

Before purchasing, request a written breakdown identifying:

- Whether VAT applies
- Whether VAT is included in the advertised price
- Other applicable national taxes
- Developer processing charges
- Transfer-related expenses

Do not assume every house-and-lot or condominium purchase is subject to the same VAT treatment.

## 11. Annual Property Tax vs. Property Transfer Taxes

It is important to understand the difference.

| Tax | Annual or One-Time? | Main Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Real Property Tax | Annual | Property ownership |
| SEF Tax | Annual | Local education fund |
| Capital Gains Tax | Transaction-based | Certain property sales |
| Documentary Stamp Tax | Transaction-based | Taxable transfer documents |
| Transfer Tax | Transaction-based | Local ownership transfer |
| Estate Tax | Transaction-based | Transfer after death |
| Donor's Tax | Transaction-based | Property donation |

Annual taxes are generally recurring obligations.

Transfer taxes and fees are associated with specific transactions.

## 12. How to Pay Real Property Tax in Cebu

The general payment process is straightforward.

### Step 1: Obtain Your Tax Declaration

Check your property's Tax Declaration from the appropriate City or Municipal Assessor.

Verify the property's assessed value and classification.

### Step 2: Request a Real Property Tax Assessment

Visit the local Treasurer's Office and request the current tax computation.

Ask for outstanding balances, penalties, interest, and applicable discounts.

### Step 3: Verify Your Payment Period

Local governments commonly allow advance payment and installment arrangements subject to applicable laws and local ordinances.

Under the Local Government Code, the basic annual RPT may generally be paid in four quarterly installments.

Check the applicable due dates and discount provisions with your LGU.

### Step 4: Pay at the Authorized Collection Office

Depending on the LGU, payment may be available through:

- City or Municipal Treasurer
- Authorized payment centers
- Local government online payment facilities
- Other authorized collection channels

### Step 5: Keep Your Official Receipt

Retain your official receipt as proof of payment.

You may need it when selling, transferring, or refinancing your property.

## 13. What Happens If You Do Not Pay Property Taxes?

Failure to pay Real Property Tax can result in penalties, interest, and collection proceedings.

Under the Local Government Code, delinquent real property tax generally carries interest of 2% per month on the unpaid amount, subject to the statutory maximum.

Persistent nonpayment can result in levy and auction proceedings in accordance with applicable legal procedures.

If you have unpaid property taxes, request a statement of account from your local Treasurer's Office.

Do not rely on an informal computation when settling tax arrears.

## 14. How to Reduce Your Property Ownership Costs Legally

Property owners can manage their expenses by understanding available payment arrangements and maintaining accurate records.

Consider the following:

**Pay within the applicable discount period.**

Some LGUs offer discounts for advance or prompt payment under their local ordinances.

**Keep your property records updated.**

Ensure that the Tax Declaration accurately identifies the property and its improvements.

**Verify the assessed value.**

If you believe the assessment is incorrect, ask the Assessor's Office about the formal assessment review and appeal procedures.

**Check available tax exemptions.**

Certain properties and property owners may qualify for exemptions under applicable laws.

**Maintain complete tax records.**

Keep official receipts, clearances, and ownership documents for future transactions.

Never use undervaluation or inaccurate property declarations to avoid taxes.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Property Taxes in Cebu

### How much is the annual property tax in Cebu?

The amount depends on the property's assessed value, classification, and local tax rates. The basic RPT rate is subject to the statutory maximum of 2% in cities and 1% in provinces, with a separate SEF levy generally set at 1%.

### How is real property tax calculated in Cebu City?

The computation uses the assessed value multiplied by the applicable basic RPT rate, plus the SEF levy and any other applicable local charges.

### Do I pay property tax on the market value of my house?

Not directly. RPT is calculated using the assessed value, which is based on the property's applicable fair market value and assessment level.

### Who pays property tax when buying a house in Cebu?

The existing owner is generally responsible for property taxes accrued during their ownership period. Buyers and sellers should agree on how current-year taxes and any outstanding obligations are settled at closing.

### How much is Capital Gains Tax when selling property in Cebu?

For real property classified as a capital asset, the general rate is 6% of the applicable higher tax base, generally the gross selling price or government valuation.

### How much is Documentary Stamp Tax on a house sale?

The general DST rate for a taxable real property sale is 1.5% of the applicable tax base.

### Is Transfer Tax the same throughout Cebu?

No. Transfer Tax depends on the relevant province, city, or municipality and its applicable ordinance.

### What happens if I do not pay real property tax?

Unpaid taxes may accumulate interest and penalties and can eventually result in legal collection proceedings, including levy and auction.

### Do condominium owners pay Real Property Tax in Cebu?

Yes. Condominium unit owners generally have RPT obligations based on the applicable assessment of their unit and associated taxable interests.

### Do OFWs need to pay property taxes in Cebu?

Yes. Filipino citizens living overseas who own taxable property in Cebu remain responsible for applicable property taxes and other obligations.

### Can I pay property taxes online in Cebu?

Payment options vary by LGU. Check the official website or Treasurer's Office of the city or municipality where the property is located.
